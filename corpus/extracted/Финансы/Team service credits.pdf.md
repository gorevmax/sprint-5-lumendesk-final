# Team service credit schedule

Finance circulation, effective 15 September 2026. This is the operating schedule for confirmed Team P1 service outages. Finance maintains the billing treatment in this document; Support Operations records impact and confirms the incident classification. Both teams use the same incident record. This circulation is in force and is intended for the people preparing or reviewing claims, rather than for an automatic promise in a status update.

A credit review begins with a specific affected workspace and a continuous interval of service unavailability. The existence of an infrastructure incident is useful evidence, but does not establish that every customer or every workspace suffered the same impact. Read the scope of the incident and the customer account together. The rules below apply to a confirmed P1 outage on Team, including an outage lasting exactly 120 consecutive minutes.

## Eligibility

Scope. This schedule applies to a Team subscription with a confirmed P1 service outage lasting at least 120 consecutive minutes.

Base. The percentage applies to the monthly subscription fee for the affected workspace, excluding taxes and usage add-ons.

Claim. The billing owner must submit a claim within 10 calendar days after the incident ends.

Evidence. A claim needs incident ID, workspace ID, impact interval in UTC and invoice ID.

## Settlement

Rate. For an eligible Team P1 outage lasting at least 120 consecutive minutes, the service credit is 15 percent of the monthly subscription fee.

Method. An approved credit offsets the next invoice and is not a cash payment.

Cap. Total incident credits cannot exceed 50 percent of the same monthly subscription fee.

Review. Finance and Support Operations jointly review incomplete or disputed claims.

Exclusions. Scheduled maintenance announced at least 48 hours in advance is outside this schedule.

## Preparing the claim file

Start with the incident identifier already used by the response team. Add the affected workspace, the invoice and the UTC impact interval. A support conversation may contain the customer's local clock time; retain that original observation and explain the conversion used in the review. Do not silently replace the beginning of actual customer impact with the time at which an agent first opened the conversation. Where evidence is incomplete, record the uncertainty and ask the incident owner to clarify it.

The billing owner submits the request. Another participant may report the outage, collect evidence or help explain a workflow, but those actions do not make that participant the billing owner. Link the verified billing contact rather than copying account credentials into the file. The claim deadline is measured from the end of the incident in calendar days. A claim received on time can still require clarification before a credit is approved; submission and approval are separate events.

## Worked review notes

Consider a Team workspace whose monthly subscription fee is 400 EUR before tax and whose eligible P1 outage lasted 120 consecutive minutes. Under this Finance schedule the credit calculation uses 15 percent of 400 EUR, or 60 EUR. Tax and an optional usage add-on are outside that base. The example shows how to calculate the amount once eligibility is established. It is not an instruction to pay a customer merely because a conversation contains the number 120.

For a second workspace, the report lists two separate interruptions with a period of restored service between them. Do not join those intervals just to make a continuous outage appear longer. Ask for the confirmed impact timeline and assess the request against the actual continuous interval. Likewise, a delay affecting one region should not be described as a global failure. Evidence in the public incident summary and evidence in the individual claim should describe compatible scopes.

If the same workspace has already received an incident credit in the billing month, include that approved amount when checking the monthly cap. Keep a link to the earlier approval so the reviewer can distinguish a new incident from a duplicate request. Do not create a second adjustment simply because the customer submitted the same request through a different channel. A correction to an existing review should preserve the history of the calculation and the reason for the correction.

## Settlement and communication

Once approved, the credit is applied against the next invoice. It is not a cash refund, and this process should not be used as a shortcut around a separate refund request. Tell the customer which invoice treatment was approved and which incident the decision covers. Avoid promising a payment date based on an unreviewed amount in a support conversation. The monthly cap remains 50 percent of the same monthly subscription fee, even when several eligible incidents are considered together.

Planned maintenance announced at least 48 hours ahead is excluded from this schedule. The reviewer should keep the announcement and the maintenance window with the evidence rather than infer advance notice from a calendar entry created afterwards. A maintenance label by itself is insufficient: confirm that the notice actually covered the affected service and interval. Where the customer disputes the notice or the incident scope, Finance and Support Operations review the case together.

Incomplete or disputed claims go to the joint Finance and Support Operations review. Keep the customer's question, the confirmed incident scope and the invoice calculation together so the reviewers can follow the request. If the customer submits additional evidence after the initial review, link it to the existing claim rather than opening another adjustment. Questions about this circulation are collected for the service-credit review meeting, and the claim owner communicates the agreed outcome through the original request.
